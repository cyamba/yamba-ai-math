# Exposition styles

Pick a style for the audience and goal, then keep to it within a section. Styles can be mixed
across sections of one piece.

| Style | Use when | Shape | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example first | Teaching; new definitions; combinatorics | Examples, pattern, statement, idea, proof | Too few examples; coincidences of small numbers |
| Definition, theorem, proof | Reference material; experts; formal companions | Definitions, statement, proof, remarks | Missing motivation; unexplained hypotheses |
| Guided questions (Socratic) | Tutoring; self-study notes | A sequence of questions the reader answers, with hidden answers | Leading questions that skip the hard step |
| Problem driven | Motivating a theory or a technique | A concrete problem, failed attempts, the idea that works, the general result | A problem too artificial to care about |
| Visual first | Spatial or combinatorial ideas | A picture carries the argument; the text makes it rigorous | A picture that suggests false generality |
| Narrative and history | Context; long programs such as large classifications | How the idea emerged, dead ends, who contributed | Myth-making; claims about priority without sources |
| Explorable | Parameters matter; growth and limits | Interactive page: the reader changes n or q and sees the effect | Interaction without a stated takeaway |
| Worked example with fading | Building a skill | Full worked example, then partly worked, then independent exercise | Fading too fast |
| Compare and contrast | Two notions or two sides of a bijection | Side by side, with matched examples and the map between them | Hiding where the analogy fails |
| Formal companion | Reading a Lean proof | Informal step aligned with each formal declaration | Explaining tactics instead of ideas |
| Practitioner guide | Applying a result | Inputs, conditions, outputs, procedure, pitfalls, code | Losing the conditions in the procedure |
| Research note | Experts deciding whether to care | Statement, status, novelty, idea, open questions | Overclaiming; burying the status |

For any style: state the status of what is explained at the top, compute every example, and
label every heuristic.
