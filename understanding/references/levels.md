# Levels of understanding

Two axes: **abstraction**, from concrete objects to general structures, and **rigor**, from
heuristic to formal. The ladder climbs both. An explanation can stop anywhere, and every stop
must be true at its own resolution.

## The ladder

| Level | Name | Question it answers | Typical content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L0 | Hook | Why should I care? | One sentence and one picture |
| L1 | Concrete case | What does it look like? | The smallest nontrivial example, fully worked |
| L2 | Pattern | What regularity is there? | Several computed examples, a table, the pattern in words |
| L3 | Statement | What exactly is claimed? | Definitions, quantifiers, hypotheses; one non-example per hypothesis |
| L4 | Idea | Why is it true? | The key idea in one sentence; a skeleton of three to seven moves; labeled heuristics |
| L5 | Proof | How do I check it? | The complete step-by-step argument, or a guided reading of the formal proof |
| L6 | Context | Where does it live? | Generalizations, related results, history, open questions, applications, formalization status |

## Audience profiles

| Profile | Enter at | Aim for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Curious newcomer | L0 | L2 | Few symbols, pictures, one running example |
| Student | L1 | L5 | Exercises at each level; every term defined |
| Researcher | L3 | L6 | Statement and novelty first; routine steps marked, not skipped silently |
| Formalizer | L3 | L5 | Exact definitions, edge cases, alignment of formal and informal statements |
| Practitioner applying results | L1 | L3, then applications | Hypothesis checklist, worked applications, pitfalls |
| Agent continuing the research | L3 | L6 | Status, dependencies, open obligations, reusable lemmas |

## Paths through the ladder

- **Spiral (teaching)**: L1, L2, L3, L4, back to L1 with the idea in hand, then L5.
- **Inverted (research note)**: L3, L4, L6, with L1 as a sanity box.
- **Visual first**: L0, L1 as a picture, L4 as a picture-based argument, then L3 and L5.
- **Application first**: L1 as a problem to solve, L3, a worked application, L4, then L6.

## Honest simplification

A lower level may omit, never mislead. Say what is omitted ("for simplicity we assume n is at
least 2"). When a simplification would become false, stop and move up a level instead.
