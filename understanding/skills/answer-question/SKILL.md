# Answer a question

1. **Classify** the question: what (meaning or definition), why (reason or idea), how (procedure
   or proof), what if (a variation), is it true (status), where from (source or history).
2. **Locate the source**: the repository file, the cited paper or the formal proof. Quote the
   definition or statement exactly where precision matters.
3. **Calibrate** the level from the wording. If the right answer would differ a lot by level and
   the wording does not settle it, ask one short question; otherwise answer at a middle level and
   offer a simpler or deeper version.
4. **Answer first** in one or two sentences, then support it: one computed example, one picture
   if the idea is spatial, and the key reason.
5. **State the status** of everything asserted, and separate "the source says" from "my
   explanation".
6. **Check understanding** with one quick question or a variation to try.
7. **Offer the next step**: a deeper level, a related result, or the workflow that fits
   (step-by-step, apply-theorem, visualize-math).

## Unknown territory

If the question reaches beyond what is known (an open problem, a new proof, whether a new claim
holds), say so plainly and do not improvise a proof. Give the honest partial answer (what is
known, small-case evidence labeled as evidence) and offer to route the question to the solver
workflows (`explore-angle`, `audit-theorem` in `skills/`).
