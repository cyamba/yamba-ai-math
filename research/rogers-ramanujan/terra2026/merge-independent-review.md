## Findings

1. **Boundary and overlap checks — supported.** In an ascending gap partition a close adjacent pair has sum divisible by 3; gap 0 gives `(3r+3,3r+3)` and gap 1 gives `(3r+1,3r+2)`. Two close pairs cannot share a part because their three parts then span at most two. For `l=2r`, checking single and triple conditions gives `L=3r-1`, `R=3r+4`; for `l=2r+1` it gives `L=3r`, `R=3r+6`. A boundary with adjacent pairs has `m≥l+2`: the four parity cases and the distance-two requirements give this, and the rule is sufficient. Successive singles require difference at least 2; triples of singles then span at least 4. All other triples cross at least one pair boundary, so those bounds cover them. These are local logical arguments for all indices, not an exhaustive partition check.

2. **Recovered coordinates — supported.** Between successive singles with h pairs, if h>0 the boundary bounds give `s'−s≥R(l_last)−L(l_first)≥5+3(h−1)=3h+2`; h=0 gives the same base separation 2. Before the first single, q pairs give level of the last at least `2q−2`, hence value at least `R(2q−2)=3q+1` when q>0; the case q=0 uses positivity. Thus mu is nonnegative and weakly ascending. For successive pairs with h intervening singles, h=0 is immediate. For h>0, the span of singles forces `L(m)−R(l)≥2(h−1)`. If `m−l≤h+1`, the bound `L(m)−L(l)≤2(m−l)` and `R(l)−L(l)≥5` force the contradictory upper bound `2h−3`; thus `m−l≥h+2`. For h singles before the first pair, `L(l)≥2h−1` and `L(l)≤2l−1` imply `l≥h`; the h=0 case follows from nonnegative level. This establishes nu's nonnegativity and weak increase. Empty vectors require no first-coordinate claim.

3. **Nonadjacent competing atoms and inverses — supported.** The key issue is that the next atom of the competing type need not be adjacent. At a state with i singles and j pairs emitted, if the literal next atom is a single S and the next pair lies after h≥1 singles, its true level is hypothetical l+h, while the last of those singles is at least S+2(h−1). Applying `L(l+h)≤L(l+1)+2(h−1)` forces the single branch. If the literal next atom is a pair at l and the next single comes after h≥1 pairs, its true value is S+3h. The last pair has level at least l+2(h−1), and its right bound is at least `R(l)+3(h−1)`; hence `S≥R(l)−3=L(l+1)+1`, forcing the pair branch. The exhausted-type cases are forced without comparing thresholds. Induction therefore recovers any valid gap atom word. In the other direction, selection at adjacent single–pair boundaries is made against `L(l+1)` before the single increments i, making the emitted pair's level l+1; pair–single selection gives `S>L(l+1)`, and the emitted single is S+3, hence at least `R(l)`. Same-type boundaries follow from the ascending inputs. Extraction then returns the original coordinates, including zeros. There is no infinite loop because each step consumes exactly one coordinate.

4. **Weight and implementation — supported subject to normal input validation.** Every pair P_l has sum `3(l+1)`. A single/pair pair contributes exactly once either to the single's count of earlier pairs or to the pair's count of earlier singles, yielding the cross term 3ab. The remaining sums give `a²+3ab+3b²+sum(mu)+3sum(nu)`. The Python `ClusterData` constructor enforces nonnegative integer lengths, exact tuple lengths, nonnegative integer entries and weak ordering; `is_gap` checks sorted positivity, both gap conditions, and integer types. The two endpoints implement the stated thresholds and coordinate formulas. The empty `ClusterData(0,0,(),())` and empty gap list each traverse no iterations and map to each other. This is an endpoint within KR1, not the product-to-cluster bridge.
