# Positive energy forces a change of particle count

**Proved, author-only; paper argument, not a fourth construction version.**
Consider any proposed full extension of frozen B2's C-cone/R20 map to
R2star. Every C object `(1,0,(w-1,),())`, w>=1, is already the image of
the unique R20 object whose nonground word is1^w. This follows directly
from B2's m=n=t=0 branch and the zero-energy grouping rule.

For every primary n_c with n>=1 and c in1..4, R2star contains a different
object consisting of its single parity encoding and terminal0_00. Its
weight is w=9n+alpha[c], and its raw energy is positive. It cannot be
sent to that same single-particle C object in an injective extension.
Thus a positive-energy decoder cannot send every one-primary-color word
to a=1,b=0, and cannot just absorb its raw energy into the sole mu entry.
This is a universal obstruction; its first case hasweight10. It does not
refute a parser that changes a,b and uses an intrinsic context.

More precisely, for nonground word1^k and raw total1 there is a unique
mixed-model object: primary P(1,1), followed by the canonical raw-zero
mixed object for1^(k-1). Raw-one nonground secondary11 is forbidden by
parity. The remaining raw-zero mixed sequence has a single initial
P(0,1) exactly when k-1 is odd, and all other atoms are S(0,1,1).
The comparison rules force that order. Therefore the corresponding
R2star is1_01 followed by that intrinsic raw-zero block and terminal.
This is an exact positive-energy grammar, not a full C assignment.

The unoccupied C objects `(2,0,(3,k+2),())` have matching weight9+k,
but assigning this family would be another seed choice. It has not been
frozen, tested or added to B2; no fourth construction version is allowed.
The observation isolates the next context decision without pretending
that a favorable equal-weight family supplies a universal parser.
