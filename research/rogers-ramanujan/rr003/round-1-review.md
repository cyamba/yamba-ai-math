## Five prioritized findings

1. **Incomplete — governing objective remains open.** The first missing target is C=(2,0,(2,2),()), of weight 8; U=(8) is a valid uncovered column object because its decoded height is 4 and the terminal drop is 4<5. The rejected input is correctly outside the advertised restricted domain, so this is not a counterexample to the restricted theorem. Independently, at weight 30 the restricted image contains 37 of 195 actual U objects. A universal carry would still leave a>=3, global coverage and endpoint implementation/proofs unresolved. No finite receipt may be promoted to complete KR1 success.

2. **Supported — the written restricted bijection is mathematically sound at ordinary manual-review assurance.** The support tests are disjoint: with no ones full support belongs to a=0; with ones full support separates by top multiplicity >=2 versus 1; the one-hole support is distinct even for b=0. Empty objects and ones-only objects supply the stated separate branches. Nonnegative multiplicity differences invert the suffix sums exactly, including zero-padding. Intrinsic a,b are recovered on S_U, without row-count assumptions or stored history. The decoded-height increments for successive 3j are 1 or 2; the only skipped level has height difference 3 or 4. The bottom heights are 2, optional 1, or 3 in the b=0 one-hole case, so all terminal drops remain <5. The baseline sums and telescoping identity establish the stated weights for arbitrary b, not just tested b. Thus both inverse identities and coverage of precisely S_U follow without a counting premise. The suffix, final-mu and merge/split operations preserve their claimed data and exact costs; no iteration rule for arbitrary common mu increments has been proved.

3. **Refuted only for the maximum-only continuation — no impossibility of Mechanism A is established.** For b=0, the proposed top-only steps pass from 6 to 8 to 10, but U=(10) has terminal height 6 and is invalid. U=(10,1) also fails, with height drop 6−1=5. For b=1, raising the case-four top 9 to 11 yields a value outside F's image. These are precise obstructions to that update rule. The decisive next lemma is a recoverable cost-two carry taking mu=(k,v) to (k+1,v+1), preserving b and nu, with valid images whose branches are intrinsically recognizable and disjoint from every already assigned sector. It must handle k=1 before any claim to a general extension. A pivot is a justified allocation choice if that lemma cannot be supplied; it must not be described as a proof that the entire core route fails.

4. **Conditional — Mechanism B has an attributed target grammar, not a matched recursion.** Chern–Li v3, section 4.1, Claims 4.2–4.3 give seven tails and their linking sets. Definition 2.5 makes each branch reconstruction a tail union a translated smaller partition, with the weight change including shift times residual length. The matching residue-side branch coding, recoverability of auxiliary state and universal disjointness/exhaustiveness remain missing. Equal univariate series or recurrence equations do not recover branch choices. A useful next test is the smallest incompatibility of an explicitly specified branch match; failure of a length-preserving or same-cutoff match cannot refute all recursive constructions. [Chern–Li primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/1809.08655v3).

5. **Supported within the stated restricted scope — cost and attribution boundaries are appropriate.** For unary weight N, at most N output entries, values and decoded heights O(N), and quadratic conjugation scans give a conservative O((N+1)^2 log(N+2)) bit-work bound; O((N+1)^2) auxiliary bits safely covers lists, counters and sets. Enumeration is not map work. This bound is not yet a bound for a universal bijection. Kurşungöz Theorem 7 explicitly supplies the attributed cluster decomposition and base weight, while Andrews–Keith section 2 and Theorem 6 supply the attributed flat-map and weighted setting. I inspected those statements and relevant construction passages, not every imported endpoint proof; RR002's transcription warning remains relevant. The elementary restricted embedding may already be known. **Novelty status: NOT VERIFIED.** [Kurşungöz](https://arxiv.org/html/1808.01432), [Andrews–Keith](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.05202v2).
