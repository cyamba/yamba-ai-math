## Eligibility, measures and the normalization qualification

Every omitted band prime uses at least L^a_i of log H and has reciprocal at most exp(-L^a_i). This proves (1), uniformly for H comparable to x. The usual prime harmonic estimate gives full-band mass log 2+o(1); hence the eligible V_i are bounded above and below, with V_i<1 eventually. The c_V estimate and the harmlessness of its indicated power follow.

For a fixed state index set and a fixed good projection, replacing normalizers changes the physical state measure by the global scalar c_V^m and each operational edge by c_V. The weighted adjoint changes by that same edge scalar, since the state-measure change is global. The endpoint moment then changes by exactly c_V^(m+2R). This applies to the common eligible model, or to an operator compressed at every state to unit first coordinate. It does not by itself compare the old full-band ambient B with the eligible ambient B: a nonunit intermediate state can carry a list prime dividing H even when the selected root is unit. A two-state illustration has a unit state e, a removed state a, and old B exchanging e and a; its old root moment at R=1 is one and its restricted root moment is zero, irrespective of scalar weight rescaling. The remedy is to state the common-index-set hypothesis explicitly. The actual candidates already use eligible groups at every intermediate state.

The distinction between compatible mass s_i and the whole memory alphabet is retained correctly. At fixed z the line of a newly appended pair is forced to [z]_p, so its prime sum is s_i. The full pair alphabet has mass sum_p(p+1)nu_i(p), often much larger, but no operation sums a fresh unconstrained line from that alphabet. Incompatible existing coordinates are unchanged by G and do not add a large mass factor.

## G and E adjoints at the actual factorial measure

For one compatible memory type, let n be its occupation and write a=-eps/theta, b=-eps*V_i/theta, c=q/theta^2. A G transition removing d and appending l has coefficient

    binom(n,d) * a^d * c^(n-d) * (b*nu)^l/l!.

Multiplication by the source/target occupation-measure ratio `(nu^n/n!)/(nu^(n-d+l)/(n-d+l)!)` produces precisely the reversed transition, removing l and appending d with a and b interchanged. This proves the claimed adjoint, including repeated coordinates. Summing absolute values with the Schur weight v0^k gives (7) and (8). Their parenthetical factors are at most one by the stated choice of theta,v0 and V_i<1. Their exponentials involve only s_i. Products over groups remain bounded, and the cap reduces the positive majorants.

The E adjoint can likewise be checked without a free projective-line factor. In one group let p be the departing active prime, q the incoming one, S indicate storing p, and I indicate recalling q. Cross-edge exclusion gives p!=q. If n is the source memory occupation, then n'=n+S*e_p-I*e_q. Its forward operational coefficient, apart from geometry and damping, is

    n_q^I * V_i^(-I) * nu(q)^(1-I).

Multiply by the active-list ratio nu(p)/nu(q) and the memory occupation-measure ratio. The result is

    nu(p)^(1-S) * (n_p+1)^S * V_i^(-I).

The reversed canonical memory operation has this coefficient times V_i^(I-S). Thus the actual adjoint is the reversed rule times V_i^(S-I), bounded above and below by fixed constants. The stored line is anchored at the source position and the recalled line at the target. The damping counts reverse with those positions. There is no sum over an arbitrary extra line, and no p+1 factor is omitted.

For each fixed determinant the Bezout parameterization leaves O(1) targets in the tau strip. The comparison kernel has O(Y) reduced determinants and size O(L^(3A0)/Y). Coordinate recalls cost at most N_mem^K. This proves the coarse row and column bounds (9), with the just-derived adjoint and the actual state measures. Bounded symmetric choice multipliers preserve these absolute majorants. Such a statement alone would not preserve a small signed norm; the author makes the correct distinction.

## Prime lifetimes, memory tail and distinctness

For a fixed physical path, a prime's independent-line factor is exactly (16). Shared primes remain continuously active; cross-edge exclusion prevents an unshared departing prime from immediately being freshly reselected. Thus each maximal consecutive active run incurs one physical V_i^(-1) normalizer. A gap between runs must retain the same memory coordinate under global introduction distinctness and recover it with the next V_i^(-1) factor. A first introduction through a list has nu_i(p), while a first ghost introduction followed by recall has V_i*nu_i(p) times that first recall factor. In either case the total coefficient is V_i^(-number_of_runs)/((p+1)b'_p), with the required prefix/suffix damping. This checks the run normalization rather than treating every active time as a new draw.

For an inactive prime there is either no introduction or one ghost lifetime. Grouping the elementary expansion by its first and last selected deficit gives the stated lifetime sum. The edge theta factors cancel the G retention factor to q_j; the birth and removal factors give the stated epsilon signs and V_i factors. Under global distinctness, ordered distinct births cancel their append factorials exactly. Empty initial and final memory remove unmatched factors. Multiplying these finite local identities proves (17).

For the cut tail, exceeding N_mem after a factor requires at least N_mem-2KR G introductions. Marking each by two is therefore valid. A never-active prime has one birth and one removal; the positive sum over possible later removal times telescopes and costs O(R/p), without a free line count. Their product is at most exp(O_K,rho(R)) because the reciprocal group masses are bounded. The union of active primes is only O_K(J+R): each edge replaces at most one slot per group. Their lifetime choices contribute exp(O_K((J+R)log R)), on top of the physical path/list coefficient bound. This proves (18), with an L^2 negative exponent dominating all listed subpower costs. The argument is correctly restricted to globally distinct introductions; it does not assert this product bound for unrestricted repeated ghosts.

The capped introduction universe and its activity flags are finite. The cycle-sign identity (19), its rank definition and the transposition encoding give the weighted rank count. For high rank, prescribing a genuinely fresh E atom costs Delta; prescribed G coordinates retain their append factorial and give the same atom cost. These bounds are uniform even when an earlier prescribed value is another coordinate of that tuple. Initial repeated-value constraints have the same cost. Iterating the absolute row bound from the empty-memory endpoint gives the stated high-rank decay with its chosen r0.

For low rank, all phases attached to a G birth tuple are collected before averaging it. The continuation summed over all future histories is symmetric in that tuple: permute its memory-coordinate labels in every later subset and recall choice. Repeated indices, length caps and fresh-versus-recall activity flags are preserved. Later births keep their own indices. Thus the finite group average changes neither the matrix element nor its active-index conditions. The modified G factors retain their absolute Schur bounds. At most 2r E factors carry a marked birth index and need only the coarse bound; every other E is literally unchanged and retains its signed norm on arbitrary input vectors, including vectors affected by earlier phases. This proves the low-rank product bound. Since |B| is polynomial and r0/R tends to zero at the displayed rate, all its counting costs are L^o(R). Together with the cap and high ranks, this proves (6).
