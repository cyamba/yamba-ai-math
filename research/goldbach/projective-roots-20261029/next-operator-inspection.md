## Proposed arithmetic-coordinate issue to verify

For a primitive both-unit root P=(u,v), start from an original complement (r,s) with u*s-v*r=1 and 0<=r<u. The noncanonical determinantH basis [P,H*(r,s)] spans the sector lattice containing endpoints Q with det(P,Q) divisible by H. In these coordinates the condition that Q's first coordinate be a unit modulo H is simply that its first relative coordinate be a unit.

Canonicalizing the second column instead gives c_H=H*r-q*u in [0,u), q=floor(H*r/u), and d_H=H*s-q*v. Then G_H=[[u,c_H],[v,d_H]] has determinantH, as in the active brief. A relative point z in this canonical basis has original first coordinate

    u*z1 + c_H*z2 = u*(z1-q*z2) modulo H.

Thus the corresponding unit condition becomes gcd(z1-q*z2,H)=1. The quotient q depends on the arithmetic root and has up to H possible values. Verify this exact algebra and its role in source moment paths before using it. The local prime-line prescriptions in ROOTS-008 are coprime to H and do not, by themselves, average this additional root-dependent condition.

A possible next method is to retain that condition as a diagonal projection, using norm<=1 in auxiliary-operator estimates. But it requires an actual proof that the arithmetic root replacement and moment bound remain valid with that projection. Dropping a projection from an alternating path expansion is not automatically a pointwise or moment inequality. Conversely, its large modulus is not by itself a proof of impossibility; a norm or positive-semidefinite domination argument may avoid averaging it directly.
