The [degree-zero proof](degree-zero.md) now supplies a **complete written
base-case construction**, rather than a matching search. A state is determined
by its flattened ordered part word A and consecutive-path cuts K, with
`F subset K subset P(A)`, where F is the graph-component gap set and P(A)
is the part-boundary set. Toggling the first boundary in `P(A)\F` reverses
sign, preserves degree and selects the same boundary on return. Its only
positive survivor exists precisely when the graph-component sizes sort to
the target partition. Independently, actual q-zero colorings give
`X_G(x;0)=product_C e_(|C|)(x)`. This is established elementary base-case
mathematics, not a novel solution of the positive-degree target or formalization.

An extension preserving A at every degree is **refuted by a written finite
cardinality proof**. On

`h=(2,4,4,6,7,7,7)`, `A=(3,3,1)`, `M=9`, `D=1`,

there are **11 negative states and 10 positive states**. The
[nine-block exhaustion](partword-obstruction.md) proves the complete raw
sector polynomial

`C_A(q)=-q [2]_q^2 [3]_q`.

Its degree-one first difference is minus one. Even unrestricted matching
inside that fixed A cannot cover the negatives. This obstructs the proposed
invariant, not the complete grammar, which can change A, and not elementary
unimodality. The actual coloring-checked coefficient is `q^3(1+q)^3`, whose
degree-one first difference is zero. The missing capacity comes from other
ordered composition sectors. Smallest here means among the four disclosed
development cases ordered by `n,h,D,A`, not a global minimality claim.
