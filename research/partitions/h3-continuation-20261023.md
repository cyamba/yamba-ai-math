## P011 first author proposal: the adjacent-color map

This section is a written construction awaiting independent challenge and
Lean checking. It is not a proved polynomial theorem.

Fix adjacent colors `a,a+1` and an actual proper coloring of a height-two
natural unit interval graph. Let `U` be vertices in these two colors and
let their multiplicities be `s,t`. Existing proofs give `s,t <= 2`.

1. If `s=t`, use the identity coloring. Its monomial is invariant under
   swapping the variables even though the individual coloring is unchanged.
2. If one class is empty, exchange the two colors on all of `U`.
   There are no internal edges. Comparisons with every outside color are
   unchanged because the two exchanged colors are adjacent.
3. Otherwise unequal multiplicities are `1,2`. The two equally colored
   vertices form a chain `u <_P v`. Height two prevents the remaining vertex
   `z` from being comparable to both. The induced graph on `U` has either
   one edge and a unique isolated vertex, or two edges forming a path.
4. In the one-edge case, switch the color of the isolated vertex only.
   It belongs to the double color class, so the multiplicities exchange.
   No internal edge changes color, and outside comparisons stay unchanged.
5. In the two-edge case, exchange colors on all three vertices. Natural
   unit interval monotonicity forces `u < z < v` in vertex labels. The two
   path edges contribute one ascent before and after the exchange.

The support `U` and its induced graph remain unchanged. The isolated vertex
is defined from `U` and edges, so the same rule is selected on a second
application. The map is involutive, preserves properness/ascent degree and
exchanges only the two adjacent color multiplicities. Finite-sum reindexing
then proves adjacent-variable invariance of the actual chromatic polynomial.
Symmetry follows by generation of finite permutations by adjacent swaps.

Required formal interfaces include the structural one-/two-edge dichotomy,
properness, exact exchanged multiplicities, pointwise outside comparisons,
ascent preservation, involutivity, monomial renaming and the finite-coloring
sum bijection. Empty vertex/alphabet cases may not be excluded from the
eventual theorem. Color rows increase in color and match descents, as in
the retained two-vertex orientation regression.

After symmetry, construct the actual coloring/list-array bijection, retain
integer shifted shapes and the correct inverse-permutation sign, and sum
the existing involution. Padding removal must be a bijection with the full
existing families rather than an existential body predicate.
