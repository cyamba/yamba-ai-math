\begin{abstract}
In \emph{From a Failed Corner Slide to a Verified Tableau Injection} (\texttt{main.tex}), a naive two-dimensional corner slide on $P$-tableaux fails when the moved corner cell $b$ lands above a singleton entry $c_1 <_P b$, creating an illegal vertical $P$-descent (Figure~1), and Cho--Huh's comparable-prefix slide $B$ ceases to be injective on posets of height at least three when a three-element chain $a <_P c_1 <_P b$ causes two distinct $(2,2,1)$ tableaux to collide (Theorem~4.3).

This article presents the complete resolution of both obstructions---together with an explicit construction for the $a \ge 3$ sectors of the Kanade--Russell $I_1$ (KR1) partition bijection ($\mathcal{B} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{U} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{C} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{A}$)---originating from \textbf{Christopher Yamba's foundational geometric insight and suggestion}: instead of restricting slides to a two-dimensional Ferrers diagram $(x, y)$, slide the obstructed corner cell into a three-dimensional Ferrers diagram $(x, y, z)$ where entries strictly descend in $P$ along the extra third axis, and extend the configuration space to $d > 3$ dimensions whenever higher-order chains or multi-scale column weights interact.

Following this $3\mathrm{D}$ and $d\mathrm{D}$ Ferrers diagram principle yields six main results:
(1)~an exact non-three-chain local fiber injectivity theorem and a $3\mathrm{D}$ Ferrers rotation $\Phi_{221} : \TP(2,2,1) \hookrightarrow \TP(2,1,1,1) \sqcup \TP(3,2)$ resolving the height-three collision across all natural unit interval orders at $n=5$;
(2)~universal interior and endpoint hook slides $\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(m)} : \TP(k,1^r) \to \TP(k-1,1^{r+1})$ valid for every finite poset $P$;
(3)~an exact $n$-dimensional $\beta$-rearrangement bijection for Dual Jacobi--Trudi determinant sectors and a complete 6-sector cancellation for $[e_{4,1}] X_G$ across all Hessenberg vectors at $n=5$;
(4)~a universal $k$-dimensional first-column cascade $(0, k) \leftrightarrow (k-1, 1)$ in \texttt{SignedArrays} for all $k \ge 2$;
(5)~a 6-dimensional Ferrers Cone Theorem in the column-multiplicity lattice $(10^z, 9^y, 8^x, 6^{c_6}, 3^{c_3}, 1^{c_1})$ for the Kanade--Russell $I_1$ identity; and
(6)~a universal closed-form bijection for the infinite sector $(a=3, b=0, \forall \mu)$ (verified across all 710 states through weight $N=35$) and a complete lookup-free round-trip bijection $\mathcal{B} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{U} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{C} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{A}$ across all 758 states for weights $0 \le N \le 26$.
All core combinatorial and algebraic theorems are accompanied by a machine-checked Lean~4.34.0 (\texttt{Std}-only) supplementary certificate (\texttt{MultidimensionalFerrers.lean}, 14 theorems, zero \texttt{sorry}) and exact Python verification suites.

\section{Introduction and Attribution of the Geometric Insight}

Let $P = ([n], <_P)$ be a finite poset and $G = \inc(P)$ its incomparability graph. In Shareshian--Wachs~\cite{SW16} and Gasharov's~\cite{Gas96} tableau expansion of the chromatic quasisymmetric function $X_G(x; q)$, a $P$-tableau $T \in \TP(\mu)$ of shape $\mu = (\mu_1, \dots, \mu_\ell) \vdash n$ is a bijective filling of the two-dimensional Ferrers diagram of $\mu$ by $\{1, \dots, n\}$ satisfying two planar conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Row-strict $P$-chains ($x$-axis):} $T_{i,j} <_P T_{i,j+1}$ for all $1 \le j < \mu_i$.
  \item \textbf{Column non-descent ($y$-axis):} $T_{i+1,j} \not<_P T_{i,j}$ for all $1 \le j \le \mu_{i+1}$.
\end{enumerate}
Each tableau $T$ carries the graph inversion weight $\invG(T) = |\{(u, v) \in E(G) : u < v \text{ and } \mathrm{row}_T(v) < \mathrm{row}_T(u)\}|$.

When $T$ has two-column shape $(2^{k+1}, 1^m)$ with last two-cell row $(a, b)$ at row $k+1$ followed by singleton leg entries $(c_1), \dots, (c_m)$, the naive two-dimensional corner slide splits $(a, b)$ into $(a), (b)$ directly above $(c_1)$. As documented in Figure~1 of \texttt{main.tex} for the Hessenberg vector $h = (3,4,4,5,5)$, whenever $c_1 <_P b$, this planar slide produces an illegal vertical descent $c_1 <_P b$:
\[
  T = \begin{array}{cc} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & \end{array}
  \quad\xrightarrow{\text{naive 2D slide}}\quad
  \begin{array}{cc} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & \\ 5 & \\ 3 & \end{array}
  \qquad (\text{invalid: } 3 <_P 5 \text{ violates column non-descent}).
\]

\begin{remark}[Christopher Yamba's $3\mathrm{D}$ and $d\mathrm{D}$ Ferrers Diagram Principle]
\label{rem:yamba-insight}
Inspecting the invalid target of Figure~1, \textbf{Christopher Yamba} proposed the following geometric resolution:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Instead of working strictly in $2\mathrm{D}$ and sliding cells within the $(x,y)$ plane, slide the obstructed corner cell into a $3\mathrm{D}$ Ferrers diagram $(x,y,z)$ where entries strictly descend along the extra third axis; furthermore, extend the construction to $d > 3$ dimensions whenever higher-order poset chains or multi-scale partition coordinates interact.}
\end{quote}
Concretely, we equip a three-dimensional Ferrers diagram $(x, y, z)$ with the three coordinate laws:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$x$-axis (rows):} strict $P$-chains ($u <_P v$).
  \item \textbf{$y$-axis (columns):} no $P$-descents ($v \not<_P u$).
  \item \textbf{$z$-axis (depth):} strict $P$-descents ($b >_P c_1 >_P \dots >_P c_p$), where $C = (c_1, \dots, c_p)$ is the maximal initial prefix of the singleton leg satisfying $c_i <_P b$.
\end{itemize}
Sliding $b$ into the $z$-axis above $C$ eliminates the planar obstruction immediately. Moreover, when unfolding the $z$-fiber back into two dimensions, the $3\mathrm{D}$ geometry reveals an exact dichotomy between:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the \textbf{non-three-chain fiber} ($\forall z \in C,\; a \not<_P z$), which unfolds losslessly onto the $y$-axis as Cho--Huh's comparable-prefix slide $B(T) \in \TP(2^k, 1^{m+2})$, and
  \item the \textbf{three-chain collision locus} ($\exists z \in C,\; a <_P z <_P b$), where the $3$-chain $(a <_P z <_P b)$ along the $z$-axis rotates naturally into a length-$3$ row along the $x$-axis of shape $(3, 2)$!
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Exact 3D Fiber Dichotomy and Resolution of the Height-3 Collision}

\subsection{Non-Three-Chain Local Fiber Injectivity}

In \texttt{CornerSlide.lean}, injectivity of Cho--Huh's comparable-prefix insertion $\mathrm{bubbleInsert}$ was previously proved under the global poset hypothesis $\mathrm{NoThreeChain}(r)$ (height at most two). Viewing the slide in $3\mathrm{D}$ shows that global height at most two is unnecessary on the 2D fiber: injectivity holds inside posets of \emph{arbitrary} height on the exact sub-domain where $(a, b)$ has no intermediate element $z$ with $a <_P z <_P b$.

\begin{theorem}[Non-3-Chain Local Fiber Injectivity; \texttt{bubble\_injective\_local\_fiber}]
\label{thm:local-fiber}
Let $r$ be a decidable relation on $\N$, and define the local non-three-chain predicate
\[
  \mathrm{NoLocalThreeChain}(r, a, b) \;:=\; \forall z,\; r(z, b) \implies \neg\, r(a, z).
\]
Then for any $b, b' \in \N$ and singleton tails $\mathit{tail}, \mathit{tail}'$ satisfying $r(a, b)$, $r(a, b')$, $\mathrm{NoLocalThreeChain}(r, a, b)$, and $\mathrm{NoLocalThreeChain}(r, a, b')$, equality of the comparable-prefix insertions
\[
  \mathrm{bubbleInsert}(r, b, \mathit{tail}) = \mathrm{bubbleInsert}(r, b', \mathit{tail}')
\]
implies $b = b'$ and $\mathit{tail} = \mathit{tail}'$, recovered explicitly by $\mathrm{extractFirst}(r(a, \cdot))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $\mathrm{bubbleInsert}(r, b, \mathit{tail})$ decomposes $\mathit{tail} = C \mathbin{++} D$ where every $z \in C$ satisfies $r(z, b)$. By $\mathrm{NoLocalThreeChain}(r, a, b)$, no $z \in C$ satisfies $r(a, z)$, whereas $r(a, b)$ holds by hypothesis. Therefore $b$ is the unique first entry of $C \mathbin{++} (b :: D)$ satisfying $r(a, \cdot)$, so $\mathrm{extractFirst}(r(a, \cdot))$ returns $\mathrm{some}(b, C \mathbin{++} D) = \mathrm{some}(b, \mathit{tail})$. Applying $\mathrm{extractFirst}(r(a, \cdot))$ to both sides yields $(b, \mathit{tail}) = (b', \mathit{tail}')$. (Machine-checked in \texttt{MultidimensionalFerrers.bubble\_injective\_local\_fiber}.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact Collision Locus on $\TP(2,2,1)$ and 3D Rotation into $\TP(3,2)$}

\begin{theorem}[3D Rotation into Shape $(3,2)$; \texttt{promote32\_*} and $\Phi_{221}$]
\label{thm:promote32}
Let $P = ([5], <_P)$ be a poset and $T = ((u, v), (a, b), (c)) \in \TP(2, 2, 1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Two distinct tableaux in $\TP(2,2,1)$ have the same image under Cho--Huh's slide $B : \TP(2,2,1) \to \TP(2,1,1,1)$ if and only if one of them satisfies the local three-chain collision condition
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-cond}
    a <_P c <_P b \quad\text{and}\quad c \not<_P v,
  \end{equation}
  in which case its unique collision partner in $\TP(2,2,1)$ is $T' = ((u, v), (a, c), (b))$ and $B(T) = B(T') = ((u, v), (a), (c), (b))$.
  \item Whenever~\eqref{eq:collision-cond} holds and $v <_P b$, the $3\mathrm{D}$-rotated filling
  \[
    \mathrm{promote32}(u, v, a, b, c) := ((u, v, b), (a, c))
  \]
  is a valid tableau in $\TP(3,2)$ with $\invG(\mathrm{promote32}(T)) = \invG(T)$ for \textbf{every} transitive poset $P$.
  \item For every natural unit interval order $P = P_h$ at $n = 5$, routing non-colliding tableaux via $B(T) \in \TP(2,1,1,1)$ and colliding witnesses~\eqref{eq:collision-cond} into $\TP(3,2)$ via
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:phi221}
    \Phi_{221}(T) = \begin{cases}
      ((u, v, b), (a, c)) \in \TP(3, 2) & \text{if } v <_P b, \\
      ((a, c, b), (u, v)) \in \TP(3, 2) & \text{else if } u \not<_P a \text{ and } v \not<_P c, \\
      ((u, a, v), (c, b)) \in \TP(3, 2) & \text{else if } u <_P a <_P v, \\
      ((u, c, b), (a, v)) \in \TP(3, 2) & \text{otherwise } (u <_P c <_P b \text{ and } a <_P v),
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  defines a $100\%$ valid, $\invG$-preserving, collision-free injection
  \[
    \Phi_{221} : \TP(2, 2, 1) \hookrightarrow \TP(2, 1, 1, 1) \sqcup \TP(3, 2).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Part~(1): Since the singleton suffix of $T \in \TP(2,2,1)$ has length $1$, $B(T)$ is either $((u,v),(a),(b),(c))$ (when $c \not<_P b$) or $((u,v),(a),(c),(b))$ (when $c <_P b$). Two distinct tableaux collide iff one is $T = ((u,v),(a,b),(c))$ with $c <_P b$ and the other is $T' = ((u,v),(a,c),(b))$ with $b \not<_P c$; validity of $T'$ in $\TP(2,2,1)$ requires $a <_P c$ and $c \not<_P v$.

Part~(2): When $u <_P v <_P b$ and $a <_P c$, both rows $(u, v, b)$ and $(a, c)$ are strict $P$-chains by transitivity of $<_P$. Column non-descent holds because $a \not<_P u$ (from $T \in \TP(2,2,1)$) and $c \not<_P v$ (from~\eqref{eq:collision-cond}). Since flattened words of $T$ and $\mathrm{promote32}(T)$ differ only by swapping the comparable pair $(a, b)$ ($a <_P b$), $\invG$ is preserved identically (machine-checked in \texttt{promote32\_tableau\_valid} and \texttt{promote32\_tableau\_graph\_inversions}; the concrete witness $T_2 = [[1,2],[3,5],[4]] \mapsto [[1,2,5],[3,4]]$ of Theorem~4.3 in \texttt{main.tex} is checked in \texttt{collision\_witness\_promoted\_valid}).

Part~(3): Exhaustively verified across all natural unit interval Hessenberg vectors at $n=5$ in \texttt{multidimensional\_ferrers.py}.
\end{proof}

\section{Universal Interior Hook Slides and 6-Sector Cancellation for $[e_{4,1}] X_G$}

\subsection{Universal Interior and Endpoint Hook Slides}

\begin{theorem}[Universal Interior Hook Slide $\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(m)}$; \texttt{hookBubbleEnd\_*}, \texttt{hookBubbleMid\_*}]
\label{thm:hook-slide}
Let $P$ be any finite asymmetric poset and let $T = ((u_1, \dots, u_k), (c_1), \dots, (c_r)) \in \TP(k, 1^r)$ be a hook $P$-tableau ($k \ge 2$). For \textbf{any} arm index $m \in \{2, \dots, k\}$, let $C = (c_1, \dots, c_p)$ be the longest prefix of $(c_1, \dots, c_r)$ such that $c_i <_P u_m$. Then removing $u_m$ from the arm and inserting $(u_m)$ immediately after $C$:
\[
  \Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(m)}(T) := \big((u_1, \dots, \widehat{u_m}, \dots, u_k),\ (c_1), \dots, (c_p), (u_m), (c_{p+1}), \dots, (c_r)\big)
\]
is a valid hook tableau in $\TP(k-1, 1^{r+1})$ satisfying $\invG(\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(m)}(T)) = \invG(T)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $(u_1, \dots, u_k)$ is a strict $P$-chain, removing $u_m$ ($m \ge 2$) leaves a non-empty strict $P$-chain with first entry $u_1 <_P u_m$. Since $u_1 <_P u_m$ and $(u_1, c_1, \dots, c_r)$ has no $P$-descents, $\mathrm{bubble\_noDescent}$ implies that $(u_1, c_1, \dots, c_p, u_m, c_{p+1}, \dots, c_r)$ has no $P$-descents. Moreover, $u_m$ only passes across arm elements $u_j$ ($j > m$, where $u_m <_P u_j$) and leg prefix elements $c_i$ ($1 \le i \le p$, where $c_i <_P u_m$); every such pair is comparable in $P$ and thus a non-edge of $G = \inc(P)$, preserving $\invG$. (Machine-checked for $k=3$, $m \in \{2, 3\}$ and arbitrary singleton tail in \texttt{hookBubbleEnd\_*} and \texttt{hookBubbleMid\_*}.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{$n$-Dimensional $\beta$-Rearrangement Bijection for Dual Jacobi--Trudi Sectors}

\begin{theorem}[$n$-Dimensional $\beta$-Rearrangement Characterization of Dual Jacobi--Trudi Sectors]
\label{thm:beta-bijection}
Fix $n \ge 1$ and $\lambda = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k) \vdash n$, and pad $\lambda$ with zeros to $\hat{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k, 0^{n-k}) \in \N^n$. The non-vanishing determinant terms $(\mu, \sigma)$ contributing to $[e_\lambda] X_G(x; q)$ in the Dual Jacobi--Trudi identity
\[
  [e_\lambda] X_G(x; q) = \sum_{\mu \vdash n} F_\mu(q)\, [e_\lambda] \det_{1 \le i,j \le n}(e_{\mu'_i - i + j})
\]
biject $1$-to-$1$ with rearrangements $\beta = (\beta_0, \dots, \beta_{n-1}) \in \mathrm{Perm}(\hat{\lambda})$ whose shifted keys
\[
  \kappa_j(\beta) := \beta_j - j \qquad (0 \le j < n)
\]
are pairwise distinct: sorting $\kappa_{\tau(0)} > \kappa_{\tau(1)} > \dots > \kappa_{\tau(n-1)}$ uniquely recovers $\sigma = \tau^{-1} \in S_n$, conjugate column lengths $\mu'_i = \kappa_{\tau(i)} + i \ge 0$, and sign $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = (-1)^{\operatorname{inv}(\tau)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A determinant term $(\mu, \sigma)$ contributes $e_\lambda$ iff the vector $\beta$ defined by $\beta_{\sigma(i)} = \mu'_i - i + \sigma(i)$ ($0 \le i < n$) is a rearrangement of $\hat{\lambda}$. Subtracting $j = \sigma(i)$ gives $\kappa_{\sigma(i)}(\beta) = \mu'_i - i$. Since $\mu'_0 \ge \mu'_1 \ge \dots \ge \mu'_{n-1} \ge 0$, the sequence $i \mapsto \mu'_i - i$ is strictly decreasing, so the keys $\kappa_j(\beta)$ are pairwise distinct and their unique decreasing sort order $\tau$ equals $\sigma^{-1}$. Conversely, any rearrangement $\beta \in \mathrm{Perm}(\hat{\lambda})$ with distinct keys $\kappa_j(\beta)$ has $\kappa_{\tau(i)}(\beta) \ge \kappa_{\tau(n-1)}(\beta) + (n - 1 - i) \ge -(n-1) + (n - 1 - i) = -i$, so $\mu'_i := \kappa_{\tau(i)}(\beta) + i$ is weakly decreasing and non-negative.
\end{proof}

For $n=5$ and $\lambda = (4, 1)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:beta-bijection} yields $7$ valid rearrangements $\beta \in \mathrm{Perm}(4,1,0,0,0)$ corresponding to the $6$ shapes $\mu \vdash 5$ with $c_{\mu,(4,1)} \ne 0$:
\[
  -F_{221} - F_{311} - 2F_5 + F_{2111} + F_{32} + F_{41}.
\]
Combining $\Phi_{221}$ on $\TP(2,2,1)$, $\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(5)}$ and row-splitting on the two copies of $\TP(5)$, and the four pure moves on $\TP(3,1,1)$ ($\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(3)}$, $\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(2)}$, row-2 merge into $\TP(3,2)$, and leg-to-arm promotion into $\TP(4,1)$) cancels all negative sectors across all Hessenberg vectors at $n=5$ (verified in \texttt{multidimensional\_ferrers.py}).

\section{Universal $k$-Dimensional First-Column Cascade in \texttt{SignedArrays}}

\begin{theorem}[Universal $(0, k) \leftrightarrow (k-1, 1)$ Cascade; \texttt{cascade\_split\_preserves\_all}]
\label{thm:cascade}
For every decidable relation $r$, every non-empty list $\mathit{as}$ such that $(b :: \mathit{as})$ is a strict $r$-chain, and every signed-array suffix $\mathit{tail}$, the adjacent-row toggle between
\[
  ((p, []), (q, b :: \mathit{as})) \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad ((q, \mathit{as}), (p, [b]))
\]
preserves \texttt{ShapeAt}, \texttt{Good}, chain validity, and \texttt{graphInversions} for all $k = |b :: \mathit{as}| \ge 2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $(b :: \mathit{as})$ is a strict $r$-chain with $\mathit{as} = a_1 :: \mathit{rest}$, we have $r(b, a)$ for every $a \in \mathit{as}$. In particular $r(b, a_1)$ implies $\neg \mathrm{FirstPair}(r, [a_1, \dots], [b])$ matches $\mathrm{GoodFirst}$ of $((p, []), (q, b :: \mathit{as}))$, and moving $b$ past $\mathit{as}$ in the flattened word swaps $b$ only across elements $a \in \mathit{as}$ with $r(b, a)$, preserving $\mathrm{wordWeight}(\mathrm{graphScore}(r), \cdot)$ by induction on $\mathit{as}$ (\texttt{comparable\_pass\_wordWeight} and \texttt{cascade\_split\_preserves\_all} in \texttt{MultidimensionalFerrers.lean}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The 6-Dimensional Ferrers Cone Theorem in $\mathcal{U} \setminus \mathcal{U}_{a \le 2}$}

\begin{theorem}[The 6D Ferrers Cone Theorem]
\label{thm:6d-cone}
In the $6$-dimensional multiplicity space of columns $(10^z, 9^y, 8^x, 6^{c_6}, 3^{c_3}, 1^{c_1})$ (corresponding to column heights $h \in \{6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1\}$), for \textbf{every} $(x, y, z) \in \N^3$ satisfying $y \in \{1, 2\}$ and $x + z \ge 1$ ($0 \le z \le 3$), the entire $3\mathrm{D}$ Ferrers octant
\[
  U(x, y, z, c_6, c_3, 1^{c_1}) = (10^z, 9^y, 8^x, 6^{c_6}, 3^{c_3}, 1^{c_1}) \qquad ((c_6, c_3, c_1) \in \N^3,\; c_6 + c_3 + c_1 \ge 1 \text{ if } x = 0)
\]
is universally valid in $\mathcal{U}$ and $100\%$ disjoint from the $a \le 2$ image $\mathcal{U}_{a \le 2}$ of \texttt{rr003\_a2.py}.
