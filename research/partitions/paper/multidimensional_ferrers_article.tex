\begin{abstract}
In \emph{From a Failed Corner Slide to a Verified Tableau Injection} (\texttt{main.tex}), a naive two-dimensional corner slide on $P$-tableaux fails when the moved corner cell $b$ lands above a singleton entry $c_1 <_P b$, creating an illegal vertical $P$-descent (Figure~1), and Cho--Huh's comparable-prefix slide $B$ ceases to be injective on posets of height at least three when a three-element chain $a <_P c_1 <_P b$ causes two distinct $(2,2,1)$ tableaux to collide (Theorem~4.3).

Christopher Yamba's geometric suggestion---to explore a third Ferrers coordinate and higher-dimensional extensions---motivates the constructions studied here. We prove a local fiber-injectivity lemma, conditional tableau rotations, a general hook-slide argument, a signed-array cascade, and a corrected $n$-dimensional $\beta$-rearrangement bijection. Exact Python enumeration verifies the complete routing maps and selected Dual Jacobi--Trudi cancellations for all $42$ natural unit interval orders at $n=5$ and all $132$ at $n=6$.

For Kanade--Russell $I_1$ (KR1), we record weight identities, proposed cone and sector rules, and a finite round-trip check across $5{,}461$ states of weights $0\le N\le40$. The implementation uses an explicit finite apex table; it is not a lookup-free construction or a proof of an unrestricted KR1 bijection. The supplementary Lean certificate proves specified local lemmas and arithmetic identities, not the complete routing algorithms, the $\beta$ theorem, or the general KR1 construction. Revision 2 corrects the rotation selection and determinant permutation convention and separates these assurance levels.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Attribution of the Geometric Insight}

Let $P = ([n], <_P)$ be a finite poset and $G = \inc(P)$ its incomparability graph. The tableau expansions of Gasharov~\cite{Gas96} and Shareshian--Wachs~\cite{SW16} apply under their respective hypotheses; the $q$-weighted applications here use natural unit interval orders. Independently, a $P$-tableau $T \in \TP(\mu)$ of shape $\mu = (\mu_1, \dots, \mu_\ell) \vdash n$ is a bijective filling of the two-dimensional Ferrers diagram of $\mu$ by $\{1, \dots, n\}$ satisfying two planar conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Row-strict $P$-chains ($x$-axis):} $T_{i,j} <_P T_{i,j+1}$ for all $1 \le j < \mu_i$.
  \item \textbf{Column non-descent ($y$-axis):} $T_{i+1,j} \not<_P T_{i,j}$ for all $1 \le j \le \mu_{i+1}$.
\end{enumerate}
Each tableau $T$ carries the graph inversion weight $\invG(T) = |\{(u, v) \in E(G) : u < v \text{ and } \mathrm{row}_T(v) < \mathrm{row}_T(u)\}|$.

When $T$ has two-column shape $(2^{k+1}, 1^m)$ with last two-cell row $(a, b)$ at row $k+1$ followed by singleton leg entries $(c_1), \dots, (c_m)$, the naive two-dimensional corner slide splits $(a, b)$ into $(a), (b)$ directly above $(c_1)$. As documented in Figure~1 of \texttt{main.tex} for the Hessenberg vector $h = (3,4,4,5,5)$, whenever $c_1 <_P b$, this planar slide produces an illegal vertical descent $c_1 <_P b$:
\[
  T = \begin{array}{cc} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & \end{array}
  \quad\xrightarrow{\text{naive 2D slide}}\quad
  \begin{array}{cc} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & \\ 5 & \\ 3 & \end{array}
  \qquad (\text{invalid: } 3 <_P 5 \text{ violates column non-descent}).
\]

\begin{remark}[Christopher Yamba's $3\mathrm{D}$ and $d\mathrm{D}$ Ferrers Diagram Principle]
\label{rem:yamba-insight}
Inspecting the invalid target of Figure~1, \textbf{Christopher Yamba} proposed the following geometric resolution:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Instead of working strictly in $2\mathrm{D}$ and sliding cells within the $(x,y)$ plane, slide the obstructed corner cell into a $3\mathrm{D}$ Ferrers diagram $(x,y,z)$ where entries strictly descend along the extra third axis; furthermore, extend the construction to $d > 3$ dimensions whenever higher-order poset chains or multi-scale partition coordinates interact.}
\end{quote}
For the present constructions the extra coordinate is geometric bookkeeping, rather than a proved general theory of three-dimensional tableaux. Its conditions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$x$-axis (rows):} strict $P$-chains ($u <_P v$).
  \item \textbf{$y$-axis (columns):} no $P$-descents ($v \not<_P u$).
  \item \textbf{$z$-coordinate bookkeeping:} each prefix entry satisfies $c_i <_P b$; this does not assert a chain among the $c_i$. Here $C = (c_1, \dots, c_p)$ is the maximal initial prefix of the singleton leg satisfying $c_i <_P b$.
\end{itemize}
Comparable-prefix insertion moves $b$ below $C$ and avoids the original adjacent descent. The local fiber analysis distinguishes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the \textbf{sufficient non-three-chain fiber} (under the stronger global predicate $\forall z,\;z<_P b\Rightarrow a\not<_P z$), which unfolds losslessly onto the $y$-axis as Cho--Huh's comparable-prefix slide $B(T) \in \TP(2^k, 1^{m+2})$, and
  \item the \textbf{three-chain collision locus} ($\exists z \in C,\; a <_P z <_P b$), which motivates testing rotations into a length-$3$ row; validity and inversion weight must still be checked.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Exact 3D Fiber Dichotomy and Resolution of the Height-3 Collision}

\subsection{Non-Three-Chain Local Fiber Injectivity}

In \texttt{CornerSlide.lean}, injectivity of Cho--Huh's comparable-prefix insertion $\mathrm{bubbleInsert}$ was previously proved under the global poset hypothesis $\mathrm{NoThreeChain}(r)$ (height at most two). Viewing the slide in $3\mathrm{D}$ shows that global height at most two is unnecessary on the 2D fiber: injectivity holds inside posets of \emph{arbitrary} height on the exact sub-domain where $(a, b)$ has no intermediate element $z$ with $a <_P z <_P b$.

\begin{theorem}[Non-3-Chain Local Fiber Injectivity; \nolinkurl{bubble_injective_local_fiber}]
\label{thm:local-fiber}
Let $r$ be a decidable relation on $\N$, and define the local non-three-chain predicate
\[
  \mathrm{NoLocalThreeChain}(r, a, b) \;:=\; \forall z,\; r(z, b) \implies \neg\, r(a, z).
\]
Fix $b,b'\in\N$ and singleton tails $\mathit{tail}, \mathit{tail}'$ satisfying $r(a, b)$, $r(a, b')$, $\mathrm{NoLocalThreeChain}(r, a, b)$, and $\mathrm{NoLocalThreeChain}(r, a, b')$. Then equality of the comparable-prefix insertions
\[
  \mathrm{bubbleInsert}(r, b, \mathit{tail}) = \mathrm{bubbleInsert}(r, b', \mathit{tail}')
\]
implies $b = b'$ and $\mathit{tail} = \mathit{tail}'$, recovered explicitly by $\mathrm{extractFirst}(r(a, \cdot))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $\mathrm{bubbleInsert}(r, b, \mathit{tail})$ decomposes $\mathit{tail} = C \mathbin{++} D$ where every $z \in C$ satisfies $r(z, b)$. By $\mathrm{NoLocalThreeChain}(r, a, b)$, no $z \in C$ satisfies $r(a, z)$, whereas $r(a, b)$ holds by hypothesis. Therefore $b$ is the unique first entry of $C \mathbin{++} (b :: D)$ satisfying $r(a, \cdot)$, so $\mathrm{extractFirst}(r(a, \cdot))$ returns $\mathrm{some}(b, C \mathbin{++} D) = \mathrm{some}(b, \mathit{tail})$. Applying $\mathrm{extractFirst}(r(a, \cdot))$ to both sides yields $(b, \mathit{tail}) = (b', \mathit{tail}')$. (Machine-checked in \nolinkurl{MultidimensionalFerrers.bubble_injective_local_fiber}.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact Collision Locus on $\TP(2,2,1)$ and 3D Rotation into $\TP(3,2)$}

\begin{theorem}[3D Rotation into Shape $(3,2)$; \texttt{promote32\_*} and $\Phi_{221}$]
\label{thm:promote32}
Let $P = ([5], <_P)$ be a poset and $T = ((u, v), (a, b), (c)) \in \TP(2, 2, 1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Two distinct tableaux in $\TP(2,2,1)$ have the same image under Cho--Huh's slide $B : \TP(2,2,1) \to \TP(2,1,1,1)$ if and only if one of them satisfies the local three-chain collision condition
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-cond}
    a <_P c <_P b \quad\text{and}\quad c \not<_P v,
  \end{equation}
  in which case its unique collision partner in $\TP(2,2,1)$ is $T' = ((u, v), (a, c), (b))$ and $B(T) = B(T') = ((u, v), (a), (c), (b))$.
  \item Whenever~\eqref{eq:collision-cond} holds and $v <_P b$, the $3\mathrm{D}$-rotated filling
  \[
    \mathrm{promote32}(u, v, a, b, c) := ((u, v, b), (a, c))
  \]
  is a valid tableau in $\TP(3,2)$ with $\invG(\mathrm{promote32}(T)) = \invG(T)$ for \textbf{every} transitive poset $P$.
  \item \textbf{Finite computational statement, not a Lean theorem.} For every natural unit interval order $P=P_h$ at $n=5$, use the following ordered candidates:
  \[
  \begin{array}{ll}
  R_1(T)=((u,v,b),(a,c)),& R_2(T)=((a,c,b),(u,v)),\\
  R_3(T)=((u,a,v),(c,b)),& R_4(T)=((u,c,b),(a,v)).
  \end{array}
  \]
  Write $W(T)$ for condition~\eqref{eq:collision-cond}, and set
  \[
  A_i(T):\Longleftrightarrow R_i(T)\in\TP(3,2)
       \ \text{and}\ \invG(R_i(T))=\invG(T).
  \]
  The corrected construction is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:phi221}
  \Phi_{221}(T)=\begin{cases}
    B(T),&\neg W(T),\\
    R_{i_*(T)}(T),&W(T),\quad i_*(T)=\min\{i\in\{1,2,3,4\}:A_i(T)\}.
  \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  The two output shapes distinguish the disjoint-union sectors. Exhaustive enumeration checks existence of $i_*$, validity, weight preservation, and injectivity for all $42$ such $h$. Weight preservation of an accepted candidate follows from its predicate; candidate coverage and injectivity are separate finite checks.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Part~(1): Since the singleton suffix of $T \in \TP(2,2,1)$ has length $1$, $B(T)$ is either $((u,v),(a),(b),(c))$ (when $c \not<_P b$) or $((u,v),(a),(c),(b))$ (when $c <_P b$). Two distinct tableaux collide iff one is $T = ((u,v),(a,b),(c))$ with $c <_P b$ and the other is $T' = ((u,v),(a,c),(b))$ with $b \not<_P c$; validity of $T'$ in $\TP(2,2,1)$ requires $a <_P c$ and $c \not<_P v$.

Part~(2): When $u <_P v <_P b$ and $a <_P c$, both rows $(u, v, b)$ and $(a, c)$ are strict $P$-chains by transitivity of $<_P$. Column non-descent holds because $a \not<_P u$ (from $T \in \TP(2,2,1)$) and $c \not<_P v$ (from~\eqref{eq:collision-cond}). Since flattened words of $T$ and $\mathrm{promote32}(T)$ differ only by swapping the comparable pair $(a, b)$ ($a <_P b$), $\invG$ is preserved identically (machine-checked in \nolinkurl{promote32_tableau_valid} and \nolinkurl{promote32_tableau_graph_inversions}; the concrete witness $T_2 = [[1,2],[3,5],[4]] \mapsto [[1,2,5],[3,4]]$ of Theorem~4.3 in \texttt{main.tex} is checked in \nolinkurl{collision_witness_promoted_valid}).

Part~(3): Exhaustively verified across all natural unit interval Hessenberg vectors at $n=5$ in \nolinkurl{multidimensional_ferrers.py}; this is computational evidence for this fixed size. The earlier displayed branch predicates were incorrect. For $h=(2,2,4,5,5)$ and $T=((1,4),(2,5),(3))$, $\invG(T)=1$, whereas $R_2(T)=((2,3,5),(1,4))$ has weight $2$. The corrected predicate rejects $R_2$ and selects $R_4(T)=((1,3,5),(2,4))$, of weight $1$. The Lean theorem for $R_1=\mathrm{promote32}$ retains its explicit hypotheses and does not establish the complete routing map.
\end{proof}

\section{Universal Interior Hook Slides and 6-Sector Cancellation for $[e_{4,1}] X_G$}

\subsection{Universal Interior and Endpoint Hook Slides}

\begin{theorem}[Universal Interior Hook Slide $\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(m)}$; \texttt{hookBubbleEnd\_*}, \texttt{hookBubbleMid\_*}]
\label{thm:hook-slide}
Let $P$ be any finite asymmetric poset and let $T = ((u_1, \dots, u_k), (c_1), \dots, (c_r)) \in \TP(k, 1^r)$ be a hook $P$-tableau ($k \ge 2$). For \textbf{any} arm index $m \in \{2, \dots, k\}$, let $C = (c_1, \dots, c_p)$ be the longest prefix of $(c_1, \dots, c_r)$ such that $c_i <_P u_m$. Then removing $u_m$ from the arm and inserting $(u_m)$ immediately after $C$:
\[
  \Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(m)}(T) := \big((u_1, \dots, \widehat{u_m}, \dots, u_k),\ (c_1), \dots, (c_p), (u_m), (c_{p+1}), \dots, (c_r)\big)
\]
is a valid hook tableau in $\TP(k-1, 1^{r+1})$ satisfying $\invG(\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(m)}(T)) = \invG(T)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $(u_1, \dots, u_k)$ is a strict $P$-chain, removing $u_m$ ($m \ge 2$) leaves a non-empty strict $P$-chain with first entry $u_1 <_P u_m$. Since $u_1 <_P u_m$ and $(u_1, c_1, \dots, c_r)$ has no $P$-descents, $\mathrm{bubble\_noDescent}$ implies that $(u_1, c_1, \dots, c_p, u_m, c_{p+1}, \dots, c_r)$ has no $P$-descents. Moreover, $u_m$ only passes across arm elements $u_j$ ($j > m$, where $u_m <_P u_j$) and leg prefix elements $c_i$ ($1 \le i \le p$, where $c_i <_P u_m$); every such pair is comparable in $P$ and thus a non-edge of $G = \inc(P)$, preserving $\invG$. (Machine-checked for $k=3$, $m \in \{2, 3\}$ and arbitrary singleton tail in \texttt{hookBubbleEnd\_*} and \texttt{hookBubbleMid\_*}.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{$n$-Dimensional $\beta$-Rearrangement Bijection for Dual Jacobi--Trudi Sectors}

\begin{theorem}[$n$-Dimensional $\beta$-Rearrangement Characterization of Dual Jacobi--Trudi Sectors]
\label{thm:beta-bijection}
For the $X_G$ application assume $P$ is a natural unit interval order; the determinant-term bijection itself is algebraic and does not require a poset. Fix $n \ge 1$ and $\lambda = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k) \vdash n$, and pad $\lambda$ with zeros to $\hat{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k, 0^{n-k}) \in \N^n$. The non-vanishing determinant terms $(\mu, \sigma)$ contributing to $[e_\lambda] X_G(x; q)$ in the Dual Jacobi--Trudi identity
\[
  [e_\lambda] X_G(x; q) = \sum_{\mu \vdash n} F_\mu(q)\, [e_\lambda] \det_{1 \le i,j \le n}(e_{\mu'_i - i + j})
\]
biject $1$-to-$1$ with rearrangements $\beta = (\beta_0, \dots, \beta_{n-1}) \in \mathrm{Perm}(\hat{\lambda})$ whose shifted keys
\[
  \kappa_j(\beta) := \beta_j - j \qquad (0 \le j < n)
\]
are pairwise distinct: sorting $\kappa_{\tau(0)} > \kappa_{\tau(1)} > \dots > \kappa_{\tau(n-1)}$ uniquely recovers $\sigma = \tau \in S_n$, conjugate column lengths $\mu'_i = \kappa_{\tau(i)} + i \ge 0$, and sign $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = (-1)^{\operatorname{inv}(\tau)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A determinant term $(\mu, \sigma)$ contributes $e_\lambda$ iff the vector $\beta$ defined by $\beta_{\sigma(i)} = \mu'_i - i + \sigma(i)$ ($0 \le i < n$) is a rearrangement of $\hat{\lambda}$. Subtracting $j = \sigma(i)$ gives $\kappa_{\sigma(i)}(\beta) = \mu'_i - i$. Since $\mu'_0 \ge \mu'_1 \ge \dots \ge \mu'_{n-1} \ge 0$, the sequence $i \mapsto \mu'_i - i$ is strictly decreasing, so the keys $\kappa_j(\beta)$ are pairwise distinct and their unique decreasing sort order $\tau$ equals $\sigma$. Conversely, any rearrangement $\beta \in \mathrm{Perm}(\hat{\lambda})$ with distinct keys $\kappa_j(\beta)$ has $\kappa_{\tau(i)}(\beta) \ge \kappa_{\tau(n-1)}(\beta) + (n - 1 - i) \ge -(n-1) + (n - 1 - i) = -i$, so $\mu'_i := \kappa_{\tau(i)}(\beta) + i$ is weakly decreasing and non-negative. Its sum is
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mu'_i
 =\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\beta_j-j)+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}i=n,
\]
so it is the padded conjugate of a unique partition $\mu\vdash n$. Set $\sigma=\tau$; then
$\mu'_i-i+\sigma(i)=\kappa_{\tau(i)}+\tau(i)=\beta_{\tau(i)}$,
recovering the original rearrangement exactly. In the other direction, strict decrease of $\mu'_i-i$ recovers the original $\sigma$ and then $\mu'$. Thus the constructions are mutual inverses, including determinant multiplicity, and the sign is $(-1)^{\operatorname{inv}(\tau)}$. This is a written proof; no Lean formalization of this bijection is claimed.

For example, $n=3$, $\beta=(0,0,3)$ gives keys $(0,-1,1)$, $\tau=\sigma=(2,0,1)$, and $\mu'=(1,1,1)$. Using $\tau^{-1}=(1,2,0)$ instead produces determinant exponents $(2,2,-1)$ and is incorrect.
\end{proof}

For $n=5$ and $\lambda = (4, 1)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:beta-bijection} yields $7$ valid rearrangements $\beta \in \mathrm{Perm}(4,1,0,0,0)$ corresponding to the $6$ shapes $\mu \vdash 5$ with $c_{\mu,(4,1)} \ne 0$:
\[
  -F_{221} - F_{311} - 2F_5 + F_{2111} + F_{32} + F_{41}.
\]
Combining $\Phi_{221}$ on $\TP(2,2,1)$, $\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(5)}$ and row-splitting on the two copies of $\TP(5)$, and the four pure moves on $\TP(3,1,1)$ ($\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(3)}$, $\Psi_{\mathrm{hook}}^{(2)}$, row-2 merge into $\TP(3,2)$, and leg-to-arm promotion into $\TP(4,1)$) cancels all $323$ negative tableaux across all $42$ Hessenberg vectors at $n=5$, leaving $259$ positive survivors matching $[e_{4,1}] X_G(q)$ degree-by-degree.

\section{Universal $k$-Dimensional First-Column Cascade in \texttt{SignedArrays}}

\begin{theorem}[Universal $(0, k) \leftrightarrow (k-1, 1)$ Cascade; \nolinkurl{cascade_split_preserves_all}]
\label{thm:cascade}
For every decidable relation $r$, every non-empty list $\mathit{as}$ such that $(b :: \mathit{as})$ is a strict $r$-chain, and every signed-array suffix $\mathit{tail}$, the adjacent-row toggle between
\[
  ((p, []), (q, b :: \mathit{as})) \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad ((q, \mathit{as}), (p, [b]))
\]
preserves \texttt{ShapeAt}, \texttt{Good}, chain validity, and \texttt{graphInversions} for all $k = |b :: \mathit{as}| \ge 2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $(b :: \mathit{as})$ is a strict $r$-chain with $\mathit{as} = a_1 :: \mathit{rest}$, we have $r(b, a)$ for every $a \in \mathit{as}$. In particular $r(b, a_1)$ implies $\neg \mathrm{FirstPair}(r, [a_1, \dots], [b])$ matches $\mathrm{GoodFirst}$ of $((p, []), (q, b :: \mathit{as}))$, and moving $b$ past $\mathit{as}$ in the flattened word swaps $b$ only across elements $a \in \mathit{as}$ with $r(b, a)$, preserving $\mathrm{wordWeight}(\mathrm{graphScore}(r), \cdot)$ by induction on $\mathit{as}$ (\nolinkurl{comparable_pass_wordWeight} and \nolinkurl{cascade_split_preserves_all} in \nolinkurl{MultidimensionalFerrers.lean}).
\end{proof}
The named Lean theorem states equivalences of \texttt{ShapeAt} and \texttt{Good}, and equality of inversion weight. It does not assert that either signed array is a globally good tableau; both local two-row configurations fail the full \texttt{Good} predicate.\par

\section{6D Ferrers Cones and the Kanade--Russell $I_1$ Bijection}

\subsection{Scale-3 Self-Similarity and Universal Head-Offset Identities}

In the Kanade--Russell $I_1$ (KR1) identity ($\mathcal{B} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{U} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{C} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{A}$), a cluster configuration $C = (a, b, \mu, \nu) \in \mathcal{C}$ has base weight $Q(a, b) = a^2 + 3ab + 3b^2$ when $\mu = 0$ and $\nu = 0$.

\begin{theorem}[Scale-3 Self-Similarity and 6D Octant Weight Identities; \texttt{kr1\_*}]
\label{thm:kr1-identities}
For all $c, r, b \in \N$ and $a \ge 1$:
\begin{align}
  Q(3c + r, b) &= r^2 + 3r(b + 2c) + 3\,Q(b, c), \label{eq:self-sim}\\
  Q(3c, b) &= 3\,Q(b, c), \label{eq:scale3}\\
  Q(a, b) &= 3b(b + 1) + a^2 + 3(a - 1)b, \label{eq:head-offset}\\
  3(b + 2) + 3(b + 1) + 3b(b + 1) &= Q(3, b), \label{eq:a3-core}\\
  (9 + 2r_2) + 6q_2 + 3d_3 + d_1 &= Q(3, 0) + 3d_3 + 2(3q_2 + r_2) + d_1. \label{eq:octant}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All five identities are arithmetic identities in $\N$ under the stated hypotheses, machine-checked in \nolinkurl{MultidimensionalFerrers.lean} (\nolinkurl{kr1_scale3_self_similarity}, \texttt{kr1\_scale3\_divisible}, \nolinkurl{kr1_head_offset_identity}, \texttt{kr1\_a3\_core\_weight}, and \nolinkurl{kr1_a3_b0_octant_weight}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The 6-Dimensional Ferrers Cone Conjecture in $\mathcal{U} \setminus \mathcal{U}_{a \le 2}$}

\begin{conjecture}[The 6D Ferrers Cone Conjecture]
\label{thm:6d-cone}
In the $6$-dimensional multiplicity space of columns $(10^z, 9^y, 8^x, 6^{c_6}, 3^{c_3}, 1^{c_1})$ (corresponding to column heights $h \in \{6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1\}$), for \textbf{every} $(x, y, z) \in \N^3$ satisfying $y \in \{1, 2\}$ and $x + z \ge 1$ ($0 \le z \le 3$), the entire $3\mathrm{D}$ Ferrers octant
\[
  U(x, y, z, c_6, c_3, 1^{c_1}) = (10^z, 9^y, 8^x, 6^{c_6}, 3^{c_3}, 1^{c_1}) \qquad ((c_6, c_3, c_1) \in \N^3,\; c_6 + c_3 + c_1 \ge 1 \text{ if } x = 0)
\]
is universally valid in $\mathcal{U}$ and $100\%$ disjoint from the $a \le 2$ image $\mathcal{U}_{a \le 2}$ of \texttt{rr003\_a2.py}.
\end{conjecture}
\begin{remark}[Evidence and remaining obligations]
The proposed argument observes that column-height differences $\Delta h$ between adjacent distinct parts in $\{6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1\}$ are at most $4 < 5$. Whenever $h = 5$ (weight $9$) appears, it is followed by at least one shorter column ($x > 0$ or $c_6 + c_3 + c_1 \ge 1$), avoiding the forbidden terminal height $5q + 5$. Furthermore, in \texttt{rr003\_a2.py}, any state containing $9$ (height $5$) either has no $\{8, 10\}$ markers ($x = z = 0$) or has $y = 1$ with markers in $\{17, 19\}$; thus $y \in \{1, 2\}$ together with $x + z \ge 1$ lies completely outside $\mathcal{U}_{a \le 2}$. The terminal and image-exclusion arguments still require a complete proof from the definitions of $\mathcal U$ and the lower-sector image. Finite membership and exclusion checks cover $3{,}744$ states in \nolinkurl{rr004_multidim_cones.py}.
\end{remark}

\section{Assurance Levels and Remaining Research}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Machine-checked local statements:} the Lean certificate proves local fiber injectivity, rotations under their explicit hypotheses, hook slides for arm length $3$, a signed-array cascade, and arithmetic weight identities. It does not prove the complete $\Phi$ algorithms or a KR1 bijection.
\item \textbf{Written arguments:} the general hook-slide statement and the corrected $\beta$-rearrangement theorem have mathematical arguments above. The latter is additionally tested by permutation round trips; it is not a Lean theorem.
\item \textbf{Finite computational verification:} complete routing and cancellation concern $n=5,6$ only; KR1 round trips concern $0\le N\le40$ only. These checks are exact but are not general proofs.
\item \textbf{Unproved general claims:} unrestricted cone membership/exclusion and the global KR1 map require proofs of totality, validity, disjointness, inverse behavior and exhaustiveness. The code's \texttt{OCTANT\_APEX\_PAIRS} is an explicit finite lookup table of apex assignments, not a formula for every apex. Extending the table is research, not a consequence of the weight identities. In fact the current rules already fail at $N=41$ with a known apex: $C=(5,0,(0,1,2,6,7),())$ maps to $U=(19,12,6,3,1)$, but \texttt{decompose\_full} returns $(2,2,(5,5),(0,1))$. Thus the all-weight extension of the current rules is refuted, not merely unverified. The special $(a=3,b=0)$ sector passes an additional finite check through $N=60$ ($4{,}491$ states), which is not a general proof.
\end{itemize}
The next construction target is to repair the $+6,+3,+1$ orbit near $(19,12,6)$ while proving disjointness from the lower-sector image. Adding missing apexes alone cannot repair the exhibited collision. For tableau routing, a stronger next target is an explicit inverse and a proof of candidate coverage under natural unit interval hypotheses.
