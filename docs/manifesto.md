# Research manifesto

We want more intelligence per unit of computation, memory, energy, and money. Our central question is whether mathematical structure can make compact models and agent systems more capable and more reliable than stronger resource-matched alternatives.

This is an invitation to ambitious invention: new attention operators, geometric representations, world models, memory mechanisms, reasoning systems, and ways for researchers and agents to collaborate. The first output is a collection of precise research angles. Software follows when a question and a discriminating experiment justify it.

## Working principles

1. **Invent boldly; label honestly.** A new connection is worth recording before it is proved. Mark speculation, conjecture, proof attempts, and experimental findings separately. Novelty is an open question until a serious literature search supports it.
2. **Make the mathematical object explicit.** Name the state space, operators, information available, symmetry assumptions, task distribution, computational model, and notion of success. A slogan becomes a research question when it admits a counterexample.
3. **Preserve the difficult distinctions.** Compression of common structure must be tested against rare facts, entity bindings, causal interventions, meaningful order, and out-of-distribution compositions.
4. **Seek qualified advantages.** Prove where an architecture helps and where it fails. A restricted theorem can be important without implying universal superiority.
5. **Price the whole system.** External memory, retrieval, tool calls, verifier computation, training, and retries are resources. A small parameter count alone is not efficiency.
6. **Treat reliability as a measurable behavior.** Separate false statements, unsupported statements, abstentions, retrieval errors, composition errors, and verification errors. Agreement is not independent evidence.
7. **Keep exploration plural.** Linear algebra, geometry, probability, information theory, dynamical systems, optimization, control, combinatorics, logic, and causal reasoning are available tools. Choose mathematics that exposes a mechanism.
8. **Preserve negative results.** Failed conjectures, impossibility bounds, and well-controlled null experiments prevent repeated mistakes and can become strong papers.

## What a strong result looks like

A result connects an explicit task family to a proved mechanism, predicts a measurable advantage, survives a counterexample search, and demonstrates that advantage against credible baselines with full costs disclosed. A theorem, a benchmark observation, and a working architecture are different contributions; each should stand on its own evidence.

Our ambition is substantial. Our records must let another researcher tell exactly what has and has not been established.
