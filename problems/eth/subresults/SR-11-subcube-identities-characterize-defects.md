# SR-11. The subcube identities are the only constraints: a local linear form of CP(Q, R)

**Status: proved** (Theorem 1, informal proof below; the necessity direction is
**Lean-verified** as `EthConsequences.Subcube.subcube_identity` in
[`SubcubeObstruction.lean`](../lean/EthConsequences/SubcubeObstruction.lean); the equivalence was
also checked in exact rational arithmetic by [`subcube_system.py`](../experiments/subcube_system.py)
on 557 edge sets for $2\le R\le Q\le6$, including every optimal and slab bad set of
[SR-02](SR-02-cube-polynomial-designs.md#exact-small-cases) and one-edge modifications of them,
with no disagreement; [results](../experiments/results/subcube_system.json)).
**Novelty: not verified** (see [RANKING.md](../RANKING.md#novelty-ledger)).
**Depends on the upstream theorem: no.**

Feeds: [ETH-A03](../01-algorithmic-core/ETH-A03-point-dependent-designs.md),
[SR-10](SR-10-touched-vertices-barrier-criterion.md). Setting: [SR-02](SR-02-cube-polynomial-designs.md)
(families $(p_v)$ of degree $<R$ with $p_v(0)=1$, distinct nonzero nodes $x_t$).

## Why it matters

CP(Q, R) was a problem about $2^Q$ unknown polynomials whose feasibility is a global linear
condition ([SR-05](SR-05-stress-duality-and-products.md)). This note removes the polynomials: the
bad set of a family is the support of an **edge function** that satisfies one explicit linear
identity per $R$-dimensional subcube, and every such edge function comes from a family. So
CP(Q, R) is the problem of finding a sparse solution of an explicit, translation-invariant
linear system, and every obstruction is a linear combination of subcube obstructions.

## Definitions

For an edge $e=\{u,u\oplus e_t\}$ the **defect** of a family is $d_e=p_u(x_t)+p_{u\oplus e_t}(x_t)$;
the edge is bad exactly when $d_e\ne0$. For a set $T$ of $R$ directions the Lagrange weights at
$0$ are $\lambda^T_t=\prod_{s\in T\setminus t}x_s/(x_s-x_t)$ ($t\in T$), so that
$f(0)=\sum_{t\in T}\lambda^T_tf(x_t)$ for every $f$ of degree $<R$.

## Theorem 1 (characterization)

Let $1\le R\le Q$. An edge function $d:E(\{0,1\}^Q)\to K$ (characteristic $0$) is the defect function of a family if
and only if, for every $R$-dimensional subcube $\Gamma$ with direction set $T$,
$$\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\lambda^T_{t(e)}\,d_e\ =\ 2^R .$$

*Proof of necessity (Lean: `subcube_identity`).* Sum $p_u(0)=\sum_{t\in T}\lambda^T_tp_u(x_t)$ over the
$2^R$ vertices of $\Gamma$; for each $t$ the inner sum splits into the $t$-edges of $\Gamma$, each
contributing its defect.

*Proof of sufficiency.* Write $d_t(v)=d_{\{v,v\oplus e_t\}}$, a function on the cube that is even in
coordinate $t$, and $A_T$ for averaging over the $T$-subcube through a vertex. The identity for
the subcube through $b$ reads $\sum_{t\in T}\lambda^T_t(A_Td_t)(b)=2$, because each $t$-edge of
$\Gamma$ is counted at both endpoints. In the Fourier basis, $A_T\chi_S=\chi_S$ if $S\cap T=\varnothing$
and $0$ otherwise, so the identities say: for every $S$ and every $R$-set $T\subseteq[Q]\setminus S$,
$$\sum_{t\in T}\lambda^T_t\,\widehat{d_t}(S)=2\,[S=\varnothing].$$
If $|[Q]\setminus S|\ge R$, values $y_t$ $(t\notin S)$ whose extrapolation to $0$ from every
$R$-subset is the same number $y_0$ lie on one polynomial of degree $<R$ with value $y_0$ at $0$
(interpolate on one $R$-subset $T_0$; for a further node $s$, the interpolants on the sets
$T_0\setminus\{t\}\cup\{s\}$ agree with it at $R-1$ nodes and at $0$, hence everywhere). So there are
polynomials $h_S$ of degree $<R$ with $h_S(0)=2[S=\varnothing]$ and $\widehat{d_t}(S)=h_S(x_t)$ for all
$t\notin S$. If $|[Q]\setminus S|<R$, choose $h_S$ of degree $<R$ with $h_S(0)=0$ and these
$|[Q]\setminus S|$ prescribed values. Put
$$p_v(T)=\tfrac12\sum_{S\subseteq[Q]}h_S(T)\,\chi_S(v).$$
Then $\deg p_v<R$ and $p_v(0)=\frac12h_\varnothing(0)=1$, and the defect of the edge
$\{v,v\oplus e_t\}$ is $\frac12\sum_Sh_S(x_t)(\chi_S(v)+\chi_S(v\oplus e_t))=\sum_{S\not\ni t}\widehat{d_t}(S)\chi_S(v)=d_t(v)$,
since $d_t$ has no Fourier mass on sets containing $t$. $\square$

## Consequences

**Corollary 2 (CP as sparse linear algebra).** $\beta^*(Q,R)$ is the least value of
$\max_t\mu\{\text{edges of direction }t\text{ with }d_e\ne0\}$ over solutions $d$ of the subcube
system $Md=2^R\mathbf 1$, where $M$ has one row per $R$-subcube $\Gamma$ and the entry
$\lambda^{T(\Gamma)}_{t(e)}$ for $e\in E(\Gamma)$. The system has $\binom QR2^{Q-R}$ rows and
$Q2^{Q-1}$ columns, each edge lying in $\binom{Q-1}{R-1}$ subcubes. The solution set is an affine
space whose direction is the space of defect functions of families with $p_v(0)=0$.

**Corollary 3 (obstructions are generated by subcubes).** A set $G$ of edges can be made
simultaneously good if and only if there are no coefficients $y_\Gamma$ with $\sum_\Gamma y_\Gamma\ne0$
whose combination $\sum_\Gamma y_\Gamma w^\Gamma$ of subcube stresses ($w^\Gamma_e=\lambda^{T(\Gamma)}_{t(e)}$
on $E(\Gamma)$, as in SR-05) vanishes on every edge outside $G$. (Fredholm alternative for
$Md=2^R\mathbf1$ with $d$ supported off $G$.) So the general stresses of SR-05 never give more
than combinations of subcube stresses whose weights cancel on the bad edges; any lower bound on
$\beta^*$ is a statement about such cancellations.

**Corollary 4 (the mean defect).** For $S=\varnothing$ the Fourier form says that the average
defect in direction $t$ equals $h_\varnothing(x_t)$ for one polynomial of degree $<R$ with
$h_\varnothing(0)=2$. Hence at least $Q-R+1$ directions have nonzero mean defect, which is
Proposition 2' of SR-02 again.

## Experiments with convex relaxations (empirical)

Iteratively reweighted $\ell_1$ minimization on the subcube system, with total or worst-direction
weights, finds the optimum $(Q,R)=(5,4)$ and sparse but direction-unbalanced solutions (for
example 5 bad edges in total at $(6,5)$, against 12 for the slab), but stays above the slab's
worst-direction count for $Q\ge6$, $R\in\{3,4,5\}$. Convex relaxations of this system are not
tight for the per-direction objective; exact methods should use the system with integer
variables for the support.
